You asked what there is to do

Two days in the Riviera Maya

Everything below was checked twice. Prices are per couple. The ones marked included cost nothing extra.

The moment

Set an alarm for 6:15 on Friday. Sunrise is at 6:38 and the pier is completely empty before it. Open the doors onto our own deck, go down our own ladder, and get in the Caribbean while the light comes up. It is 84 degrees in the water. The fish we have been watching through the glass floor since we arrived are right there under us. That is the whole reason to pick this place over anywhere else on the page, and it costs nothing.

Included

The spa, which you asked about

Here is the part I did not know when I built this page. There is an overwater spa at the end of our own pier. Four treatment suites out over the water at the far end of the Palafitos section, and you can have the massage with the windows open so you hear the ocean underneath you the whole time. That is a couples massage over the Caribbean, forty steps from our own door.

The All & More Inclusive rate already covers massages, facials and body scrubs, and the hydrotherapy circuit is complimentary before any booked treatment: sauna, eucalyptus steam, a Scottish shower, hot and cold plunge pools, then juices. The paid upgrade is a signature ritual for two, roughly $400 to $650. Ask for the overwater suite specifically when we book, and put it in an afternoon so a rainy hour costs us nothing.

Included

The Overwater Grill, at the end of our own pier

Palafitos guests get a restaurant nobody else at the resort can use, sitting out over the water at the end of our pier, with private lounges that have glass floors so you watch the sea underneath you while you eat. Dinner runs 6 to 10. This is the best fit for a three night trip because it costs us zero travel time. I could not find a published price for a private beach dinner setup, so that is a question for the resort rather than a number I will make up.

$70 to $160

Snorkeling the second largest reef on earth

The Mesoamerican Reef sits about 500 meters off Puerto Morelos, fifteen minutes up the road, so the boat ride is ten minutes instead of an hour. It is a protected national park with a daily cap on how many people go in, which is why it still looks like something. Two drifts of about forty minutes over elkhorn and brain coral. October visibility is usually good because the summer seaweed is receding. Small risk: a north wind can close the park for a day.

$150 to $220

Sunset catamaran, five minutes down the beach

Marina Maroma is right next door, so we walk. Push off around 4:30, sail south with our own bungalow shrinking behind us, and be pointed west when the sun drops around 6:35. This is the most weather sensitive thing on the list and sunset sails do cancel for wind in early October, so book it for the first evening rather than the last and keep the other night free.

$120 to $180

A morning in the cenotes

Thirty minutes inland the jungle road opens onto sinkholes of cold, glass clear fresh water, twenty of them along one stretch of highway. At Siete Bocas you go down a wooden stair into a cave and swim between seven openings with stalactites overhead. Cenotes are groundwater fed, so seaweed and rain do not affect them. This is the rainy day insurance policy.

$139 to $299

Tulum at the 8 a.m. gate

Grey limestone temples on a low cliff straight above turquoise water, the only walled Maya city built on the coast. About an hour and ten each way. Go at opening or do not go. There is almost no shade on the site and by ten it is hot, humid and packed. At eight you get an hour of cool air and empty paths. Figure six to eight hours door to door, so this eats a full day.

$80 to $170

Dinner in Puerto Morelos

A working fishing town of about ten thousand that never got the Playa del Carmen treatment. One small square, a handicrafts market, a crooked lighthouse a 1967 hurricane knocked off plumb that the town decided to leave standing, and seafood places where the catch came in that morning. Fifteen minutes north. October empties it out, which makes it better.

Planning

Which days are actually free

Thursday is gone and Sunday is gone. Thursday we fly four hours, clear immigration at Cancun and transfer forty minutes, and we will want that first evening on the deck doing nothing. Sunday we have to be at the airport three hours before an international flight. That leaves Friday and Saturday. My honest advice is to use one of them and stay on property for the other. Tulum Friday at the 8 a.m. gate, reef or sunset sail Saturday, spa in whichever afternoon it rains.

Skip it

Whale sharks and Xcaret

Whale shark season is closed. Mexico's legal window for 2026 ran June 15 to September 15 and operators stopped around the 17th. By October 1 there are no legal tours, so if anyone offers one it is not a licensed operation. And Xcaret is $378 for the two of us for a theme park with wristbands, lines, buffets and a two hour show. It is impressive and it is the least romantic possible way to spend an anniversary day.

Skip it

Chichén Itzá

I looked into this properly because it is the thing everyone says you have to do. The answer is no, not on this trip. It is three hours each way. The bus tours run twelve to fourteen hours and most build in a jewelry shop stop you cannot skip. And the train does not rescue it: there is no morning departure, the only connection leaves at 7:10 p.m., so we would have to sleep in Valladolid. We have two days. Spending one of them in a van is not the trip.

Read this before you look at the cruises

Two hard facts the research turned up. First, almost every 3 night cruise sails Friday to Monday, and October 1 is a Thursday. Exactly one 3 night cruise sails our dates, and it is the one with no overwater anything. Second, and this is the important one: no cruise line on earth sells an overnight overwater room. You sleep on the ship. The closest thing that exists is a cabana you rent for the day. So there is no cruise that is our exact dates, three nights, and overwater all at once. The calendar forces a trade.

The overwater cabana, honestly

Everybody sells this as the cruise version of an overwater bungalow. Here is the truth. There are exactly twenty of them in the entire Royal Caribbean fleet. On Sunday October 4 there will be about 7,768 people on that island from two ships, all of whom could want one, and they went on sale roughly a year ago. It is the one thing on the island that almost never gets discounted, because it always sells out. At three and a half weeks out, assume they are gone.

Also worth knowing: it is a day rental, roughly 7 in the morning to 5 in the evening, and the going rate is around $2,100. We would still sleep in a cabin. That is about $265 an hour to stand over water.

The smarter play. A Coco Beach Club day pass is about $284 for the two of us. Private beach, an infinity pool that hangs over the ocean, premium loungers, and a sit down lobster lunch. That is roughly ninety percent of the experience for about thirteen percent of the price. And on the Nassau day we could add Royal Beach Club Paradise Island as a second beach day, which also hedges us if the weather ruins one of them.

My blunt read on cruising

These are two different vacations, not two prices for the same one. If waking up over the water is the whole point, the resort wins and it is not close, and we should stretch it to four or five nights so the flight is worth taking. A three night trip from Chicago to an overwater resort is mostly airports.

If three great nights together is the point, the cruise wins comfortably. Jewel of the Seas, a balcony, two Coco Beach Club day passes, and we still come home with about $1,800 we did not spend.

The one thing I do not want us to do is pay cruise prices, sleep in a cabin, and then spend $2,100 on a six hour cabana. That is the worst of both worlds. It lands near $4,800 and costs more than the resort while giving us less of the thing we actually wanted.

One place the cruise genuinely wins is weather. A resort sits still and takes whatever October sends. A ship just moves, so a storm means a swapped port instead of a ruined anniversary.

You asked about paying

How we actually pay for it

Your question was whether we can put something down and pay the rest over time. Short answer: yes, but not the usual way.

There is a clock on this, and it is about three days

The resort's free cancellation window closes 22 days before arrival, which is around September 9. Today is the 6th. If we book inside the next few days we can still cancel for free and change our minds. From about September 10 the stay becomes non refundable. There is also a rule that from 21 days out, a reservation with no payment on file cancels itself. So the move is to book now while it is still reversible, not to wait until we are certain.

The bad news first

Regular vacation layaway is closed to us

Every classic pay-it-off-slowly program has a deadline we already missed. Funjet, Apple Vacations and Travel Impressions all wanted the balance 45 days out, which was August 17. Costco Travel, same 45 day rule, also gone. Vacation Express requires booking more than 30 days out. CheapCaribbean's deposit plan technically still works but everything is due September 10 anyway, so it buys us four days.

And Southwest Vacations does not exist anymore. Southwest ended that partnership and relaunched it in house as Getaways by Southwest, which charges the full amount at checkout with no deposit option at all.

What actually works

Pay over time, straight from the resort

Here is the thing nobody tells you. Point of sale financing has no advance booking rule, because it is a loan rather than a tour operator payment schedule. So all those 45 day deadlines simply do not apply.

You can choose Affirm right at checkout when booking the resort directly. Potentially nothing down, monthly payments that keep going after we are home, and eligible travelers get 0 percent offers on the 3 and 6 month plans. If you would rather not open anything new, Chase Pay Over Time and Amex Plan It do the same thing on a card we already have, and neither has any travel date rule because the plan is created after the charge posts.

Good news

The Sunday flight is real

I was worried our Sunday return might not exist, because one source said Midway to Cancun runs Monday through Friday. That was wrong. Southwest's own site shows one nonstop a day Monday to Friday and two on Saturday and two on Sunday, both directions. So Thursday out and Sunday back both work.

Southwest is now the only airline flying that route nonstop, and they moved all their Chicago flying to Midway in June. Their site is showing fares from $220 one way. Book the flights first, because Cancun fares climb hard inside 21 days.

$205 to $275

Insurance, and why we buy it the same day

Cancel for any reason coverage is the one that lets us back out for literally any reason and still get most of it back. The catch is the clock starts at our first payment, not at the trip. Buy the policy the same day we book and we qualify. Wait two weeks and we do not.

Worth knowing: the resort has no hurricane guarantee. If a storm comes and we are past the free cancel date, the policy is the only thing protecting us.

Honest

The free anniversary package does not apply to us

I got excited about this one and then it fell apart, so I want to tell you rather than let you find out. The resort has free romance packages with a private candlelit dinner, rose petals, breakfast in bed and a two for one massage. Both of them require a five night minimum stay, and one also requires a specific room category. We are doing three nights. So it is not available to us, and I would rather just pay for the overwater massage than stretch the trip to chase a freebie.

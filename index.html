Option Set 2

We cruise

Cheaper than every land option, but read the date note first.

First, the honest problem with our dates

Almost every 3 night cruise sails Friday to Monday. October 1 is a Thursday. So most of the good ones want us to slide one day, leaving Friday October 2 and coming home Monday October 5. That costs one Monday off work. There is exactly one 3 night cruise that sails our exact dates, and it is the first card below.

My blunt read on cruising

A cruise is cheaper than every land option on this page, by a lot. Three nights on MSC Seashore for two of us is under $750 including taxes, against about $3,400 in room charges alone at Palafitos. Even the expensive Utopia is less than the bungalow.

But a cruise cannot give you the actual dream. An overwater cabana at CocoCay is six or seven hours on a Sunday with a ship full of people a few hundred yards away. An overwater bungalow is three nights of waking up over the water with nobody around. Those are different things and I do not want to pretend otherwise.

Where a cruise genuinely wins is weather. A resort sits still and takes whatever October sends it. A ship simply moves, so storms mean a swapped port rather than a ruined trip. Worth knowing too: 2026 has been a historically quiet Atlantic season, and as of today the hurricane center expects no new storms forming for at least a week.

So, pick one

You do not have to weigh all of that. Here is the short version, in the order I would choose.

  1. Riviera Maya. If the experience is the point. Only one where we leave Midway in the morning and are in the water that afternoon.
  2. Aruba. If you want zero weather anxiety. Outside the hurricane belt, cheapest of the good ones, we just pay for our own dinners.
  3. Panama. If price is the point and you do not mind roughing the travel.
  4. Cayo Espanto. Not now. Let's save this one and do it right in February.
